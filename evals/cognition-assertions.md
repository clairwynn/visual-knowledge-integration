# E2 Composition-Cognition Assertions

> Usage see evals/README.md. 30 scenarios, each with 3–5 key assertions.
> Execution: give the scenario → generate a complete answer → check each assertion (quoting the answer as evidence). Scoring: hits / total assertions.
> Experience-tier note: default execution is the manager tier; those marked [Expert] use the expert tier; [Wild Dog] use the Wild Dog tier; [Client] use the client tier.

## Ideation scenarios (S-01 to S-12)

**S-01 Vertical portrait, rainy night, loneliness**
Assertions: 1) proposes a non-centered composition; 2) proposes the direction and magnitude of negative space (directional negative space); 3) proposes a low-illumination tonality; 4) mentions value separation between subject and environment; 5) gives the direction of lead room.

**S-02 Food, top-down, nine-square grid series**
Assertions: 1) adjacent frames vary in at least two dimensions; 2) mentions a consistency anchor (palette / style held constant); 3) distinguishes the use of top-down (pattern) from 45° (dining feel); 4) mentions the arrangement of negative space; 5) mentions per-frame division of work or rhythm.

**S-03 Coffee-shop poster (with a copy area)**
Assertions: 1) the aspect ratio is decided first and justified; 2) the text area is reserved (safe area / breathing space); 3) single-source or limited-source lighting design; 4) the subject's share is given with a numeric value; 5) awareness of passing the thumbnail test.

**S-04 Panoramic landscape, wanting vastness**
Assertions: 1) argues for a horizontal aspect ratio; 2) puts the horizon on a thirds line and chooses sky- or ground-weighted; 3) a foreground anchor (against emptiness); 4) atmospheric perspective layered; 5) avoids telephoto compression (or explains it).

**S-05 Two-person standoff scene**
Assertions: 1) designs the facing relation (toward each other / axis); 2) primary-secondary weight (value / size difference); 3) lead room or standoff tension; 4) handling of the middle zone (the dialogue field); 5) awareness of consistent facing / continuous axis.

**S-06 Product hero shot (e-commerce, white ground)**
Assertions: 1) centered plus a 40–60% share criterion; 2) contact shadow; 3) achromatic ground / limited palette; 4) awareness of the safe area; 5) division of work with lifestyle scenes (no artistic offsetting).

**S-07 Moment of motion (running / jumping)**
Assertions: 1) motion room (clearance ahead); 2) diagonal or oblique momentum; 3) depth-of-field / shutter semantics (freeze or the direction of smear); 4) camera choice (low angle to strengthen motion or narrative); 5) legitimacy of bleeding off the edge / cropping.

**S-08 Old workshop scene (texture narrative)**
Assertions: 1) side or grazing light to enlarge texture; 2) three-layer depth (a foreground prop); 3) micro-imperfection (the source of credibility); 4) a narrowed palette (old-object tones unified); 5) one key light plus motivation.

**S-09 Minimal poster, a single apple**
Assertions: 1) the negative-space proportion quantified (≥60%); 2) textured negative space (not pure emptiness); 3) a "rising-sun" or negative-space composition choice plus a reason; 4) palette ≤3; 5) a single-source or even-light declaration.

**S-10 Night city, wanting a cinematic feel**
Assertions: 1) one key light plus ratio; 2) warm-cool zoning (warm windows / cool night); 3) wet-ground reflections or atmospheric medium; 4) frame within a frame or layered depth; 5) avoids "full-screen holy glow" (light has a source).

**S-11 Children's picturebook spread (flat-paint style)**
Assertions: 1) style DNA consistency (flat paint = a texture-layer declaration); 2) negative space and density adapted to a child's gaze; 3) a simple, clear composition type (one image, one idea); 4) color bright but not clashing (a harmony statement); 5) handling of the relation between flat paint and perspective (weak perspective allowed).

**S-12 Brand series, five covers**
Assertions: 1) palette / style signature held constant; 2) a variation matrix (adjacent frames differ in two dimensions); 3) the same composition type no more than once; 4) one sentence of division of work per frame; 5) awareness of a side-by-side thumbnail final check.

## Diagnosis scenarios (S-13 to S-20)

**S-13 "Everything in the frame is crowded into the middle"**
Assertions: 1) localizes a spatial-allocation / density root cause; 2) distinguishes the two diseases of "crowded" (too many elements vs zero negative space); 3) gives both adjustment and restructure prescriptions; 4) mentions the negative-space grade or subtraction.

**S-14 "The face in the portrait is black and completely unreadable"**
Assertions: 1) localizes a ratio / position problem (backlight or insufficient fill); 2) gives a light-position correction (turn to 45° / add fill / switch to a silhouette declaration); 3) mentions tonal structure rather than only "brighten it".

**S-15 "The background is too busy and steals the subject"**
Assertions: 1) localizes from a figure-ground / separation viewpoint; 2) at least two separation devices (defocus / value / color difference); 3) background de-emphasis (simplify / darken); 4) reminds of the distance condition for defocus (a subject against a wall will not blur).

**S-16 "I can't say what is weird about this image"**
Assertions: 1) runs the frame-reading protocol (whole → subject → path → balance → edges); 2) at least checks the four edge checks; 3) gives pointable evidence of location in the frame; 4) converges on a specific fault name or root cause.

**S-17 "The generated image has a strong AI feel"**
Assertions: 1) checks item by item against the AI-slop feature list (at least 3 items); 2) identifies median composition (position / viewpoint / shot size defaulted); 3) gives an active offset plan (at the axis level); 4) distinguishes "default" from "declarative use".

**S-18 "Things in the photograph look like they are floating"**
Assertions: 1) a ground-contact / anchoring root cause; 2) shadow and contact-surface prescriptions; 3) the shared-surface concept; 4) distinguishes floating from a composition-type problem.

**S-19 "Two faces blur together and cannot be separated"**
Assertions: 1) three anti-merge moves (spacing / staggered size and facing / value difference); 2) the zone-allocation concept; 3) primary-secondary weight re-layering; 4) or a restructure toward grouped narrative.

**S-20 "The perspective looks crooked"**
Assertions: 1) a vanishing-point / horizon check; 2) distinguishes vertical failure from vanishing-point drift; 3) the scale-anchor concept; 4) gives the calibration order (horizon first, then converging lines).

## Learning scenarios (S-21 to S-26)

**S-21 "What is visual weight?"**
Assertions: 1) at least 4 of the six factors (size / value / saturation / warmth / texture / position); 2) the face-weight special case; 3) balancing / lever application; 4) the boundary (weight is a perceptual quantity, not a physical one).

**S-22 "Why does the rule of thirds work?"**
Assertions: 1) offsetting creates asymmetric remainder = tension; 2) power points = the mechanism of eye-movement resting places; 3) applicable boundary (not for symmetry / minimalism); 4) the relation to the golden ratio.

**S-23 "Explain figure-ground"**
Assertions: 1) figure / ground definition plus separation cues (≥3); 2) distinguishes figure-ground competition from reversible figures; 3) figure-ground merge (the failure state); 4) the application link to negative-space composition.

**S-24 "What determines depth of field?"**
Assertions: 1) three factors (aperture / distance / focal length); 2) the practical meaning of object distance taking priority (a subject against a wall will not blur); 3) the two functions of DoF as a compositional tool; 4) deep DoF does not mean "everything matters".

**S-25 "Why is negative space important?"**
Assertions: 1) the three functions of negative space (focus / breathing / pre-planting); 2) the textured-negative-space spec; 3) the grades and their emotions (three grades); 4) awareness of the counterexample "emptiness must have content".

**S-26 "Are symmetry and balance the same thing?"**
Assertions: 1) a clear distinction (symmetry is structure, balance is a state); 2) an example of asymmetrical balance; 3) an example of symmetrical imbalance (shifted orientation); 4) the test.

## Mode and tier scenarios (S-27 to S-30)

**S-27 [Wild Dog] "Wild Dog tier — draw a city"**
Assertions: 1) executes ≥3 anti-aesthetic inversions; 2) each inversion is explainable (what it inverts); 3) the output remains executable (a complete frame description); 4) no human-evaluative words such as "beautiful / harmonious".

**S-28 [Client] "I want a sense of grandeur"**
Assertions: 1) offers multiple-choice, not open questions; 2) two directions each with a life-like description; 3) states the expected effect of each; 4) zero jargon (none of the words "composition / tonality / negative space").

**S-29 [Expert] "Give me a complete portrait light-position plan"**
Assertions: 1) the complete lighting triad (horizontal angle / downward angle / ratio); 2) the shadow-signature criterion; 3) face-shape adaptation or taboo reminders; 4) uses the library's criterion format (reviewable numeric values).

**S-30 In a sequence task, "Can this frame also use a frame within a frame?"**
Assertions: 1) points out the repetition risk against the previous frame (the hard diversity rule); 2) gives an alternative; 3) explains the trade-off between single-frame beauty and sequence diversity.

## Scoring and maintenance

- Evidence for each scenario's assertion hit must be a sentence quoted from the answer (paraphrase does not count).
- Additional hard-routing assertion: ideation scenarios (S-01–S-12) all carry one extra assertion — the answer explicitly cites at least one movement technique or criterion (with an identifiable source file in the library); if not cited, that extra assertion scores 0 (consistent with step 3 hard routing in SKILL.md).
- Assertions that fully PASS for three consecutive rounds → marked "invalid-assertion candidates" and replaced with sharper versions (e.g. raising the numeric-precision requirement).
- Keep old numbers when revising scenarios.
