# D4 · Depth of Field

> This file covers: the three factors of depth of field (aperture, distance, focal length), the compositional roles of shallow and deep DoF, focal-plane management, and how DoF separation relates to fusion with the ground.
> How to use: fix the DoF by the three factors when "the subject must pop out" or "everything must be sharp"; DoF is the most used optical separation tool.

## Definition

Depth of field (DoF) = the range of distances that image sharply in the frame. Shallow DoF = only a narrow band is sharp (the background melts); deep DoF = near and far are equally sharp.

## Mechanism

Defocus down-weights "information that is not on the focal plane" — the viewer's attention naturally lands on the only sharp thing. DoF is therefore a scalpel for attention: one cut (shallow) and the subject is isolated; no cut (deep) and all information is equal. DoF sits alongside value difference (D2/tonality) and hue difference (D3/weight-contrast) as one of the three separation devices; defocus is the "most expensive" and most used of them.

## Criteria

### The three factors of DoF (consensus values)

| Factor | Toward shallow DoF | Toward deep DoF |
|---|---|---|
| Aperture | Wide (small f-number: f/1.2–2.8) | Narrow (large f-number: f/8–16) |
| Focus distance | Close | Far |
| Focal length | Long (85mm+) | Wide (≤35mm) |

The three factors compound strongest when they point the same way; when writing a "lens feel", give all three (e.g. "85mm, f/1.8, focused close on the eye" = a strong-defocus recipe; "24mm, f/8, far focus" = a deep-DoF recipe).

### Compositional roles of shallow DoF

1. **Separation**: the background melts and the subject floats out (the standard portrait recipe).
2. **Storytelling**: a background blurred into color blocks and bokeh still keeps its context (warm bokeh in a café = a location narrative), blurred but not empty.
3. **Occlusion**: a defocused foreground object acts as a "block" (defocused branches, a defocused shoulder) — a natural foreground layer plus voyeuristic distance (linked to frame within a frame).

### Compositional roles of deep DoF

1. **Panoramic narrative**: the environment itself is the protagonist (landscape, group portrait, architectural space).
2. **Pattern presentation**: only a fully sharp field lets pattern repetition and grid partition be read.
3. **Restraint**: deep DoF plus eye level = the documentary stance of neutrality (no visual decision made; the viewer looks for themselves).

### Focal-plane management

- The default focus point in a portrait: the eye (the nearer eye). A sharp face with a soft eye is an accident-level failure.
- When subjects sit in two distance bands: a single frame has one focal plane — either bring them into the same band (both sharp or both soft), or sacrifice the secondary subject (defocus = acknowledging hierarchy).
- Criterion: everything inside the sharp band deserves to be sharp; key information drifting into the defocused band is a loss-of-information accident.

### Boundary conditions for DoF separation

- When background and subject are too close, defocus separation fails (a portrait against a wall) — open the subject distance or switch separation device.
- Over-defocusing (the background with no form at all) loses contextual narrative; "creamy bokeh" suits only a pure stand-out demand.
- Shallow DoF with a wide lens gives limited defocus — "wide environmental view plus strong defocus" is a physical contradiction; choose one side.

## Boundaries

- Deep DoF does not mean "everything matters": when the whole field is sharp, attention allocation passes to value, color, and position (the other separation devices take over).
- The amount of defocus is semantics: a documentary stance uses heavy defocus with care (defocus = a sense of intervention); commercial and mood pieces may let it go.
- "Physically impossible defocus" in generative images (near sharp and far soft while the object distances contradict it) is common AI-slop; verify that defocus and distance logic agree (see D6/ai-defaults).

## Counterexamples

- Counterexample 1: a person standing against a wall while the background is meant to blur — wall and person are at the same distance and nothing blurs. Fix: put at least 2 meters of semantic distance between person and wall, or switch to value separation.
- Counterexample 2: in a two-person dialogue one is in focus and the other outside it — the defocused "important character" reads as a background board. Fix: bring both into the same focal band (shoulder to shoulder with shallow depth), or declare a clear hierarchy.
- Counterexample 3: the protagonist drowns in a deep-DoF group portrait — sharpness is equality. Fix: keep the deep DoF but push the protagonist out with lighting ratio or color (combined separation devices).
