# D6 · Authenticity

> This file covers: the sources of a frame's "realness" — imperfection, ordinary light, material detail, physical consistency; and the rejection criteria for photographic credibility.
> How to use: the texture and light design of realism-oriented frames; when judging "where it looks fake", check the source list item by item.

## Definition

Authenticity = the degree to which a frame reads as "a real moment". Realness comes not from "perfection" but from **disciplined imperfection**: real-world photographs contain ordinary light, accidental composition, worn surfaces, and physically consistent detail.

## Mechanism

"Perfection" is itself a signal of the artificial: real light is rarely evenly balanced, objects carry traces of use, and moments carry contingency. The viewer's intuition about "over-perfect" is exactly "fake". Authenticity design = keeping systematic traces of reality while leaving compositional control to intent (realness and designedness do not conflict: a good documentary frame is "disciplined accident").

## Criteria

### Source list for realness

| Source | Operation | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary light | Overcast, interior lamps, window light — light that is "not studio"; use hard-ratio over-perfect golden hour with care | Life happens here |
| Imperfect composition | Slight tilt (≤5°), cropping (legitimate cut positions), an accidental feel in the offset | The moment was caught, not arranged |
| Micro-imperfect surfaces | Skin pores and fine hair, pilled fabric, scratches in paint, dust | Matter has existed |
| Physical consistency | Unified shadow direction, reflections matching the environment, standing water reflecting on wet ground | The world is self-consistent |
| Incidental elements | A passer-by entering the frame (defocused), traces of wind, an insect | The moment is unrepeatable |
| Medium traces | Grain, lens flare (restrained), edge vignetting | Seen through a camera |

### Photographic credibility rejection criteria (any hit in a realistic frame deducts)

1. Shadowless light (objects look self-luminous);
2. Everything equally sharp (no focal-plane logic);
3. Over-symmetrical face with porcelain surface (plastic feel);
4. Light direction contradicting reflections (window light yet bright reflections on both cheeks);
5. Uniform material (every surface at the same roughness);
6. Floating (no ground contact, no cast shadow);
7. Cloth folds violating gravity and tension logic;
8. Perspective contradicting distance (see the four errors in D4/perspective).

Criterion: a realistic frame hitting ≥2 items has "credibility collapse"; hitting 1 marks that spot as a "fake point" and repair takes priority.

### Authenticity × style boundary

- Realism-oriented (documentary, portrait, product, landscape): the whole table applies.
- Stylized (illustration, cartoon, concept art): physical consistency may yield to style rules (a cartoon may have no shadows) — but "internal self-consistency" may not: once a rule is established within the style, the whole frame obeys it.

### The "disciplined accident" recipe (rules of thumb)

A common combination for refined realism: 1 ordinary-light item + 2 micro-imperfections + 1 incidental element, with composition still controlled precisely by D1 / D4 — to a lay viewer it is a snapshot, to a trained eye every part holds.

## Boundaries

- Imperfection is a recipe, not salt sprinkled everywhere: each imperfection must be pointable (where, and why); indiscriminate noise is just another kind of evenness.
- Authenticity is not ugliness: dirt and disorder are not realness (dirt and disorder are "errors"); the goal is "beauty that life has used".
- For "cleanliness-critical" subjects such as product shots, credibility yields to the cleanliness code (the perfection of an e-commerce hero image is industry grammar); this file's criteria are down-weighted there.

## Counterexamples

- Counterexample 1: in a realistic street shot everyone's skin is poreless and the light is perfect — "a wax museum". Fix: micro-texture the skin and give the light an ordinary source (overcast / shop lamp).
- Counterexample 2: an interior night scene with moonlight through the window yet bright reflections on both cheeks — a physical contradiction (a fake point). Fix: keep the reflection only on the source side.
- Counterexample 3: tilting the frame 10°, cutting a joint, and blurring focus all for "realness" — imperfection crossing into accident. Imperfection stays inside "micro" and "legitimate".
